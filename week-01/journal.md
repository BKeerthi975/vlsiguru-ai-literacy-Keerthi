## Date
October 8, 2026

## What I learned that was genuinely new

I learned that an LLM generates an answer one token at a time by predicting what is likely to come next. This helped me understand why a fluent and confident answer is not always a correct answer.

I also learned that some features that look like AI can actually be implemented using simple rules and logic. This made me more careful about assuming that something is AI just because it appears intelligent.

## One AI output I initially trusted

I initially trusted the source links and citations provided by AI tools for Q1, Q2, Q3, Q8, Q9 and Q10. I also initially trusted Gemini's list of Cummings guidelines for Q4.

## How I verified it

I searched for the sources and opened the links myself. I found that some links were wrong or outdated, and one paper mentioned for Q9 could not be found.

For Q4, I compared the AI-generated guidelines with the actual Cummings paper and found that one of the claimed guidelines was not present in the paper.

For Q5, I checked the HTTP 301 and 302 claims against RFC 9110 and found that 302 is "Found", while "Temporary Redirect" refers to 307.

For Q10, I checked the wire resistance values using the HyperPhysics AWG table and recalculated the voltage-drop values. The original values needed small corrections because of rounding.

I also found that some screenshot links were returning 404 errors because of a double `.png` extension, which I fixed.

## What the AI did well

The AI explained concepts clearly, helped me organize my answers and tables, and provided useful examples. It was also useful for giving me a starting point for research and helping me identify topics that needed further verification.

## What the AI could not be trusted to decide

I learned that I should not rely on AI alone to decide whether a source or link actually exists, whether an exact number is supported by a reliable source, or whether a named paper actually contains a particular statement.

An answer can look detailed and convincing while still containing incorrect or unsupported information.

## One question I still have

How are chat models trained after the basic next-token prediction stage so that they can follow instructions?

I also want to understand how a model decides between selecting the most likely token and sampling another possible token.

## What I will do differently next week

Next week, I will open and check every important link before using it. I will record verification results while I work instead of waiting until the end.

I will also check the reliability and date of each source and avoid accepting precise claims just because they are presented confidently or include a citation.

## Key Takeaway

My main takeaway from Week 01 is that AI should be treated as a useful assistant, not as the final authority. I need to verify important claims, sources and calculations before accepting or submitting them.
